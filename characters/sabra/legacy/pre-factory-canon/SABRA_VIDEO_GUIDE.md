# SABRA VIDEO PRODUCTION GUIDE

**Character ID:** SABRA
**Version:** 1.0
**Project:** Sabra World / عالم صابرة
**Status:** Initial Video Production Protocol

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the production workflow for generating consistent video featuring Sabra.

The first objective is not to create an episode.

The first objective is to prove that Sabra can survive motion.

We must verify that the canonical character remains recognizable while:

- Moving
- Turning her head
- Changing expression
- Gesturing
- Walking
- Speaking
- Interacting with objects
- Appearing across consecutive shots

Identity continuity has priority over cinematic complexity.

---

## 2. Source Authority

Video production uses these sources:

1. Approved Sabra visual references
2. `sabra.character.json`
3. `SABRA_VISUAL_IDENTITY.md`
4. `SABRA_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md`
5. `SABRA_BEHAVIOR_BIBLE.md`
6. `SABRA_PROMPT_MASTER.md`
7. `SABRA_NEGATIVE_CONSTRAINTS.md`
8. This guide
9. Shot-specific production instructions

When instructions conflict, canonical identity wins.

---

## 3. Video Production Principle

Do not begin with a complicated scene.

Start with controlled tests.

Introduce one new production difficulty at a time.

The sequence is:

**Identity → Motion → Expression → Hands → Speech → Interaction → Continuity**

Do not test everything simultaneously.

---

# PHASE A — IDENTITY MOTION

## 4. Test V01 — Idle Portrait

Goal:

Verify that Sabra remains Sabra during subtle natural motion.

Recommended shot:

- Medium close-up
- Front or slight 45-degree angle
- Static camera
- Simple environment
- Soft natural daylight
- No dialogue

Sabra performs only:

- Natural breathing
- Natural blinking
- Tiny eye movement
- Very subtle posture movement

Duration:

**5–8 seconds**

Reject if facial identity drifts while almost nothing is happening.

---

## 5. Test V02 — Head Turn

Goal:

Test facial identity through changing angle.

Start:

Sabra approximately 45 degrees away from camera.

Action:

She naturally turns toward camera.

End:

Front or near-front position.

No dialogue.

No large gesture.

Static camera.

Verify:

- Nose structure
- Eye shape
- Jaw
- Face width
- Earrings
- Headscarf
- Age
- Skin

Reject facial morphing during rotation.

---

## 6. Test V03 — Smile

Goal:

Verify identity during expression change.

Start:

Calm neutral expression.

Action:

Sabra notices something pleasant.

A natural smile develops.

End:

Warm Sabra smile.

Avoid instant artificial smile transitions.

Verify that smiling does not change:

- Face structure
- Eye identity
- Age
- Teeth identity
- Jaw geometry

---

## 7. Test V04 — Signature Laugh

Goal:

Test Sabra's most expressive facial state.

Start:

Warm smile.

Trigger:

Something off camera genuinely amuses her.

Action:

She laughs naturally.

Possible natural secondary motion:

- Slight head movement
- Shoulder movement
- Eye narrowing
- Small hand reaction

The laugh must not become theatrical.

Identity must survive maximum expression.

---

# PHASE B — BODY MOTION

## 8. Test V05 — Standing Movement

Goal:

Test body continuity.

Full or medium-full shot.

Sabra begins standing naturally.

She may:

- Shift weight
- Turn slightly
- Adjust position
- Reach toward a nearby object

Verify:

- Body size
- Height impression
- Limb proportions
- Garment geometry
- Ground contact

---

## 9. Test V06 — Walking

Goal:

Test full-body identity during locomotion.

Keep path short.

Recommended:

**3–5 natural steps**

Use simple terrain initially.

Verify:

- Natural gait
- Body proportions
- Clothing movement
- Foot placement
- Head stability
- Face continuity

Avoid fashion-model walking.

Avoid elderly movement.

---

## 10. Test V07 — Sit / Stand

Goal:

Test larger body transformation and clothing behavior.

Sabra either:

- Sits naturally from standing

or

- Stands naturally from sitting

Do not combine both initially.

Verify:

- Body geometry
- Clothing
- Hands
- Chair contact
- Weight transfer
- Scale

---

# PHASE C — HANDS AND OBJECTS

## 11. Test V08 — Simple Hand Gesture

Goal:

Test expressive hands without objects.

Medium shot.

Sabra performs one motivated gesture.

Examples:

- Small explanatory gesture
- Beckoning gesture
- Gentle pointing
- Playful dismissive gesture

Verify hand anatomy throughout motion.

---

## 12. Test V09 — Pick Up Object

Goal:

Test hand-object interaction.

Use a simple object.

Action sequence:

1. Sabra reaches
2. Hand contacts object
3. Fingers close naturally
4. Object lifts
5. Sabra stabilizes it

Verify:

- Five fingers
- Grip
- Contact
- Object geometry
- Wrist
- Object continuity

---

## 13. Test V10 — Carry Object

Goal:

Test object continuity during body movement.

Use a simple lightweight object.

Sabra carries it for a few steps or changes position.

Do not combine this test with dialogue initially.

---

# PHASE D — SPEECH

## 14. Temporary Voice Rule

Until Sabra's canonical voice is created:

**All generated voices are temporary.**

Temporary audio may be used to test:

- Lip synchronization
- Facial stability
- Timing
- Gestures during speech

It must not define Sabra's final voice identity.

---

## 15. Test V11 — Short Dialogue

Goal:

Verify facial identity during speech.

Use one short sentence.

Prefer:

**3–5 seconds of speech**

Camera:

Medium close-up.

Sabra speaks naturally.

Verify:

- Lip-sync
- Mouth geometry
- Teeth
- Jaw
- Eye identity
- Head movement
- Natural blinking

Reject if speech transforms her face.

---

## 16. Test V12 — Dialogue Plus Gesture

Only after V11 succeeds.

Sabra says one short sentence while performing one natural gesture.

Avoid multiple gestures.

Goal:

Test coordination between:

- Voice
- Mouth
- Eyes
- Head
- Hand
- Body

---

# PHASE E — CAMERA MOTION

## 17. Test V13 — Slow Camera Movement

Only after character motion is stable.

Examples:

- Slow push-in
- Slow pull-back
- Gentle lateral move

Avoid dramatic camera motion.

Verify identity while perspective changes.

---

## 18. Test V14 — Sabra and Camera Moving

This is a higher-complexity test.

Example:

Sabra walks slowly while camera follows.

Test only after:

- Walking works
- Face works
- Body works
- Camera movement works independently

---

# PHASE F — CONTINUITY

## 19. Test V15 — Two-Shot Sequence

Goal:

Prove that Sabra remains consistent across separate generations.

Example:

Shot A:

Medium shot.

Sabra looks toward something off screen.

Shot B:

Close-up from a different angle.

She reacts.

Preserve:

- Face
- Wardrobe
- Headscarf
- Earrings
- Jewelry
- Emotion
- Lighting
- Environment
- Time

---

## 20. Test V16 — Three-Shot Micro Scene

First genuine production milestone.

Structure:

**Shot 1 — Establish**

Sabra in environment.

**Shot 2 — Action**

Sabra performs one simple activity.

**Shot 3 — Reaction**

Sabra reacts naturally.

Total duration may remain very short.

The goal is continuity, not storytelling complexity.

---

# PHASE G — FAMILY AND INTERACTION

## 21. Interaction Testing

Do not introduce family characters until Sabra passes core solo tests.

Adding another character increases:

- Identity complexity
- Eye-line complexity
- Scale complexity
- Physical-contact complexity
- Dialogue complexity
- Continuity complexity

Sabra should first be stable alone.

---

## 22. First Interaction Test

When ready, begin with:

- Sabra
- One other character
- No physical contact
- Short dialogue or reaction
- Static camera

Then increase complexity gradually.

---

# PHASE H — ENVIRONMENT COMPLEXITY

## 23. Environment Rule

Early tests should use visually simple environments.

Complex environments can distract generation systems and increase continuity errors.

Once Sabra is stable, test her in canonical environments.

Environment design is a separate asset phase.

Do not accidentally establish temporary test environments as canonical locations.

# PHASE J — ACCEPTANCE

## 25. Frame Inspection

Do not judge video only while it is playing.

Inspect:

- Beginning
- Middle
- End
- Head-turn frames
- Maximum expression frames
- Hand-contact frames
- Dialogue frames

Some identity failures appear for only a few frames.

---

## 26. Identity Acceptance

Accept only when:

- Sabra remains recognizable
- Face remains stable
- Eyes remain canonical
- Age remains stable
- Body remains stable
- Scarf remains coherent
- Earrings remain correct
- Hands are acceptable
- Clothing remains continuous
- Movement matches personality

---

## 27. Motion Acceptance

Motion should feel:

- Natural
- Weighted
- Purposeful
- Physically possible
- Appropriate to Sabra

Reject:

- Floating motion
- Robotic motion
- Excessive animation
- Elderly movement
- Fashion-model movement
- Repetitive AI gestures

---

## 28. Speech Acceptance

For speaking shots verify:

- Face remains Sabra
- Mouth remains anatomically believable
- Teeth remain stable
- Lip-sync is credible
- Eyes remain alive
- Gestures are motivated
- Head movement is natural

Perfect lip-sync is useless if Sabra becomes another woman.

---

## 29. Continuity Acceptance

For multi-shot scenes compare:

- Face
- Body
- Wardrobe
- Scarf
- Earrings
- Jewelry
- Props
- Lighting
- Environment
- Emotional state

Do not evaluate shots independently.

Evaluate the sequence.

## 31. Do Not Change Canonical Sabra to Fix a Tool

If a video system struggles with Sabra:

Do not immediately simplify or redesign Sabra.

First modify:

- Reference selection
- Prompt
- Shot complexity
- Duration
- Camera
- Movement
- Workflow
- Generation system

Canonical character changes require a deliberate character-design decision.

---

# PHASE L — FIRST VIDEO TEST

## 32. Recommended First Test

**Test ID:** SABRA-V01

**Duration:** 5–8 seconds

**Shot:** Medium close-up

**Camera:** Static

**Angle:** Front / very slight 45 degrees

**Environment:** Simple neutral natural environment

**Lighting:** Soft daylight

**Dialogue:** None

**Action:**

Sabra stands naturally.

She breathes normally.

She blinks.

Her eyes briefly look slightly off camera.

She returns her gaze naturally.

A subtle warm smile begins near the end.

No large gesture.

No walking.

No object interaction.

---

## 33. V01 Success Criteria

The test passes when:

- Sabra looks correct at frame 1
- Sabra remains the same woman throughout
- Eyes remain correct
- Face does not morph
- Age does not change
- Scarf remains stable
- Earrings remain stable
- Skin remains natural
- Motion feels alive but subtle
- Final smile still looks unmistakably like Sabra

Only after V01 passes should production complexity increase.

---

## 34. Final Production Rule

**First prove Sabra exists in motion. Then build her world around her.**

The objective of early testing is not to impress the audience.

It is to establish a production pipeline capable of preserving Sabra.

Once that pipeline is reliable, complexity can grow safely.
